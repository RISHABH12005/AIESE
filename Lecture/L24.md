# Unit 6 - Ethics, Privacy, Bias, Fairness, and Explainable AI

## Scenario: AI-Based Course Recommendation System

A university develops an AI system that recommends courses to students. It uses students’ interests, completed courses, grades, and prerequisite status. The system also asks students to provide their home address. Some students notice that the AI often recommends fewer advanced courses to students who have not previously taken similar courses. The university plans to use the system to help students choose courses, but students can still consult an academic advisor.

## Part A - Ethics Check

1. **What is the main purpose of this AI system?**  
   To recommend suitable courses to students.
2. **Who could be affected by its recommendations?**  
   Students, academic advisors, and the university.
3. **What is one possible harm if the recommendation is incorrect?**  
   A student could miss a useful course or be placed on the wrong academic path.
4. **Should students be able to question or ignore an AI recommendation? Why?**  
   Yes. Students should retain choice and be able to correct errors or seek advice.

## Part B - Privacy Audit

| Data item | Needed / Not needed | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Student's course interests | Needed | Helps match recommendations to students' goals. |
| Completed courses | Needed | Shows what the student has already studied. |
| Prerequisite status | Needed | Helps check eligibility for a course. |
| Home address | Not needed | It does not help recommend courses. |

**If the system collects a home address but never uses it, is there still a privacy concern? Explain.**  
Yes. Unnecessary collection creates risks if the data is exposed, misused, or kept longer than needed.

## Part C - Privacy vs Security

1. **The chatbot asks for a student's Aadhaar number for a general course query.**  
   Privacy
2. **An attacker breaks into the database and downloads student records.**  
   Security
3. **Student information is shared with someone who does not need it.**  
   Privacy
4. **The system uses a weak password and someone gains unauthorized access.**  
   Security

**In your own words, write one difference between privacy and security:**  
Privacy concerns what information is collected and how it is used or shared; security protects information from unauthorized access.

## Part D - Bias and Fairness

1. **What pattern do you notice? (Possible bias)**  
   Students without prior similar courses are repeatedly recommended fewer advanced courses.
2. **Does this pattern automatically prove the system is unfair? Why or why not?**  
   No. It signals a possible problem, but we need evidence about student qualifications, data, and outcomes.
3. **What evidence should the university examine to check fairness?**  
   Compare recommendation rates and outcomes across relevant student groups, while accounting for grades and prerequisites.
4. **Suggest one change that could reduce the risk of unfair recommendations.**  
   Audit the model and training data for bias, and include more evidence of students' readiness than prior course history alone.

## Part E - Explainable AI (XAI)

*A student receives this recommendation: “Do not take Advanced Machine Learning.” The system provides no reason.*

1. **Why might the student find this recommendation difficult to trust?**  
   The student cannot see what information or reasoning led to it.
2. **What explanation could the AI provide to make its recommendation easier to understand?**  
   Explain which prerequisites or preparation are missing and how the student can become eligible.
3. **Should a student be able to request a human review? (Yes/No) Why?**  
   Yes. An advisor can check for errors, consider context, and discuss alternatives.

## Part F - Responsible AI: Suggest Safeguards

1. Collect only necessary data, protect it, and set clear limits on access and retention.
2. Regularly test recommendations for bias, explain decisions, and provide human review and student choice.

## Final Reflection (Optional)

- **One thing I learned about privacy is:** Collect only the data that is necessary and explain how it will be used.
- **One thing I learned about fairness is:** Recommendation patterns should be checked across student groups for unequal outcomes.
- **AI recommendations should not replace:** Human judgment, student choice, or academic advisors.
